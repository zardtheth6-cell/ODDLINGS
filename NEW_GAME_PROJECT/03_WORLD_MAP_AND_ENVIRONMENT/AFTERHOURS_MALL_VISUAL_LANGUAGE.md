# AFTERHOURS — Mall Visual Language and Art Direction

## Target
A cinematic, photorealistic-feeling abandoned regional mall. The result should resemble a carefully art-directed architectural visualization that has become playable. Realism is built from correct scale, material response, believable construction details, practical lighting, coherent signage and purposeful environmental storytelling.

## Architectural identity
Choose one consistent architectural era and design language: late-1990s / early-2000s regional mall renovated in stages. Use a coherent mix of polished stone/tile public floors, painted gypsum and block service walls, metal storefront framing, tempered-looking glass, acoustic ceiling systems, exposed service infrastructure in back-of-house and restrained decorative metalwork. Do not mix unrelated architectural styles room to room.

## Palette
- Public floors: warm grey stone, muted beige terrazzo or charcoal tile accents.
- Walls: warm off-white, aged cream, muted taupe and concrete grey.
- Metal: brushed stainless, painted dark steel and aged aluminum.
- Glass: subtle tint and believable reflections, never opaque black mirrors.
- Retail accents: each fictional tenant gets one restrained accent color.
- Emergency accents: red only on actual emergency fixtures/signage.
- Service areas: cool grey, faded safety yellow and industrial neutral.
- Light: warm neutral public fixtures, cooler service lighting, emergency backup tones where justified.

Do not globally tint the entire map blue, green or red. Use local light sources and exposure to create contrast.

## Material response
- Tile and stone: controlled roughness, visible seams, mild scuffs and localized dirt accumulation.
- Concrete: subtle tonal variation, formwork/joint logic and wear near corners.
- Painted drywall: matte response, edge wear and repair patches.
- Metal: roughness appropriate to finish, fingerprints or oxidation only where plausible.
- Glass: moderate reflection and visible thickness/frames; preserve visibility through storefronts.
- Plastic: restrained highlights; avoid toy-like shine.
- Fabric: rough matte response and slight sag where appropriate.
- Water: only where there is a believable leak, spill or feature; reflections should be localized.

Avoid over-sharpened textures, obvious tiling, stretched UVs, excessive bump/normal strength, random grunge overlays and uniformly dirty surfaces.

## Ceiling design
Ceilings are a major realism test. Public concourses need a repeatable ceiling grid with recessed fixtures, HVAC diffusers, sprinkler heads, speakers, access panels, structural beams and selected skylight zones. Retail units may have lower suspended ceilings or tenant-specific fixtures. Service areas can expose ducts, conduit, pipes and cable trays. Do not leave large blank black ceilings.

## Storefront language
Create a reusable storefront system with:
- Structural side piers.
- Sign band / fascia.
- Glass display frontage.
- Recessed entrance.
- Shutter or door system.
- Tenant-specific interior lighting.
- Signage and display props.
Vary widths, recess depth and frontage rhythm. Do not repeat identical shop fronts across the entire concourse.

## Lighting hierarchy
1. Signature atrium ambient and skylight contribution.
2. Public concourse practical ceiling lights, some failed.
3. Storefront display and tenant interior lights.
4. Service corridor fluorescent/industrial fixtures.
5. Emergency and exit fixtures.
6. Local task lights in security and maintenance.
7. Occasional motivated flicker or outage events.

Most fixtures should be off, dimmed or partly working in a designed pattern, not all randomly flickering. A flicker is an event, not a default lighting state. Critical route edges, door handles and interactables remain readable at minimum quality.

## Atmosphere and post effects
Use restrained haze only where motivated by distance, dust or a specific event. Avoid dense fog that hides navigation. Bloom must not wash out signs. Color correction must preserve material identity. Camera effects are adjustable and must not make the map unpleasant or unreadable.

## Environmental wear
Use believable wear maps:
- More foot traffic wear on concourse centerlines and entrance mats.
- Dust accumulation in corners, behind counters and on unused shelves.
- Water damage beneath a specific leak or ceiling stain.
- Scuffs around doors and loading routes.
- Rust near moisture or exposed service areas.
- Clean-ish protected surfaces where maintenance would have occurred.
Do not put identical stains on every wall.

## Original branding
Create one fictional mall identity and a consistent sign family. All tenant brands must be original. Define logo style, type hierarchy, sign dimensions, colors and placement. Use readable short labels rather than gibberish or AI-generated pseudo-text. Where reliable text rendering is unavailable, build labels with actual TextLabels or image assets.

## Final presentation
Every key zone needs:
- One establishing composition.
- One close material/detail view.
- One gameplay sightline view.
- One route/navigation view.
The atrium needs a hero view from the entrance and a reverse view from the upper balcony.
